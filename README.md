## That Sound

I was maybe class seventh aur eighth. I don't remember the exact year.

What I remember is the sound.

The first time it happened, I was just watching the Republic Day parade on TV.

I wasn’t even paying much attention until the camera tilted up - and a formation of fighter jets cut across the sky. Perfect, fast, precise. I just froze. Leaned closer without realizing it.

Something about it hit deeper than excitement. It felt like recognition.

After that, I kept going back. First just the names of those aircraft, then videos, then late-night deep dives into fighter jets, stealth tech, 5th generation aircraft, special forces, military aviation - everything.

It didn’t feel like a phase. It felt like being pulled in.

Even now, whenever I see a jet, everything goes quiet for a second.

And I know exactly when that started.

From that day I wanted one thing. The Indian Air Force 🇮🇳. The cockpit of a Sukhoi 30 MKI. That specific machine, that specific roar, those specific wings. I wanted to be the person in that aircraft protecting the sky above this country.

I worked toward it for years. Gave it everything I had.

And it didn't happen.

I'm not going to dress that up. It broke something. There are still days even now when I hear that sound overhead and my chest does that thing again and then immediately after comes this quiet ache that I don't think ever fully goes away. The ache of a door you ran toward for years and then it just wasn't there anymore.

That's the honest version.

## What I Did With It

I'm a software engineer now. I write code. I sit at a desk.

It looks nothing like what I planned.

But I've figured something out in the years since. The need to serve - the actual need, not just the dream of the cockpit or the uniform - it doesn't leave you. It sits there. And if you don't find it a way out, it just builds. I felt it building for a long time before I finally did something with it.

One night I sat down and thought - okay. I cannot fly a Sukhoi 30 MKI 🇮🇳 over Indian airspace. I cannot stand at Siachen. I cannot do any of the things I once planned. But I can build something. I can write the names down. I can make sure the stories don't disappear.

So that's what this is.

Not a portfolio project. Not something for my resume. I built this at night, alone, because I needed to. Because the love had nowhere to go and I found it a place.

This is my service. The only kind available to me now. And I have made my peace with the fact that it will look like this - civilian, small, made of code and words instead of courage and sacrifice - for the rest of my life. I will keep finding ways to fill this space. Every year, in whatever I build and whatever I do, some part of it will point in this direction. I will never stop. I genuinely do not have it in me to stop.

## The People I Think About

**Captain Vikram Batra 🇮🇳.** Twenty four years old. Kargil. He said yeh dil maange more and he wasn't joking. He went back for a wounded officer under direct fire. He didn't have to. He went anyway. I think about him more than I can explain. Something about the way he chose, in that final moment, to go back.

**Major Sandeep Unnikrishnan 🇮🇳.** 26/11. He ran into the Taj Mahal Hotel when everyone was running out. He told his men don't come up, I'll handle them. He handled it alone. He died alone in that corridor and he saved people whose names he never knew. I don't know what to do with that kind of love for other human beings. I just know I want to make sure his name stays somewhere.

**Major Shaitan Singh and the 114 men of C Company 🇮🇳.** Rezang La, 1962. Surrounded. Outnumbered maybe ten to one. Rifles freezing. No help coming. When spring came in 1963 and the snow pulled back, every single man was still at his post. Still facing the enemy. Not one had moved backward. Not one. I think about those 114 men whenever I feel like giving up on something, even something small and embarrassing compared to what they faced. I think - if they didn't move, I can finish this.

**The 40 CRPF jawans of Pulwama 🇮🇳.** February 14, 2019. They were on a bus. Going to work. A road they had taken hundreds of times. They never came home. I don't have words for it. I just have their names, and I keep them.

**The jawan standing at some border post tonight 🇮🇳** in a place most Indians will never visit, in temperatures most Indians will never experience, staring into the dark so that we don't have to. No statue. No headline. No one coming to say thank you. Just the duty and the cold and the choice he made to be there.

I carry all of them. I'll carry them my whole life.

## The Thing About the Dream Not Coming True

I used to think the dream was specifically the Sukhoi 30 MKI 🇮🇳. The wings on my chest. The rank. The exact shape of the life I had planned so carefully for so long.

I understand now that the dream was always just the direction. The uniform was one road toward it. When that road closed, the direction didn't change. Only the road did.

The purpose is older than the plan. And it will outlast every plan I ever make.

So yes, I am a software engineer who will never be a fighter pilot. That gap will live in me for the rest of my life and I know it and I have stopped pretending otherwise. But I also know that I will spend the rest of my life finding ways to fill it. This website is one way. Whatever I build next will be another. Something after that will be another. The work of filling that space never finishes and I have stopped wanting it to finish.

This is just who I am now. Someone who loved the Indian Armed Forces 🇮🇳 too much to ever let that go, and who will spend his whole life proving it in whatever ways he can. That will never change. No matter what I become, no matter where life takes me, no matter how many years pass - this part stays. This part is not going anywhere.

You're awake right now so I can sleep.

India did not forget you. I will not forget you.
